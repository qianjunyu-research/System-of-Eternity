# Bounded Propagation Without Ignition:
# Structural Limits of Local Coupling in Governance Network Simulations

## Abstract
We investigate disturbance propagation in locally coupled governance network simulations across four topologies: chain, star, random-sparse, and fully connected. The full simulator evolves trust, disturbance, stability, and cognitive distortion with local coupling, interventions, and stochastic updates; the main analysis uses a reduced disturbance-centered interpretation for clarity. Propagation is classified with a strict conjunction rule: affected share >= 20% of non-seed nodes, cascade depth >= 5, and sustained failure >= 3 consecutive steps. Baseline and weak-coupling families often show high propagation, while targeted damping, gating, nonlinear propagation, feedback, and hysteresis produce suppression regimes. Fine-grained scans over intrinsic decay (`alpha = 0.076..0.085`) reveal topology-dependent threshold windows rather than a single universal flip point. Within the tested model family and parameter ranges, we do not observe a robust sustained ignition phase as a stable attractor.

## 1. Introduction
Local interaction rules can produce rich cascades, but whether they are sufficient for sustained global ignition remains unresolved in decentralized governance-like systems. This study evaluates that question empirically using a reproducible simulation pipeline.

Research questions:

1. Can local coupling alone produce robust sustained ignition under tested conditions?
2. How does topology shift propagation extent and threshold location?
3. How do local extensions (targeted decay, gating, nonlinearity, feedback, hysteresis) reshape regime boundaries?

All conclusions are scoped to tested configurations.

Reproducibility note: `random-sparse` is not pure ER `G(N,p)`. It is constructed as a connected chain backbone plus additional random long-range edges with `extra_edge_probability = 0.06` (default in [soe_v3_topology.py](soe_v3_topology.py)).

### 2.3 Full-state simulator variables
The full simulator tracks node states:

- trust `T_i(t)`
- disturbance `D_i(t)`
- stability `S_i(t)`
- cognitive distortion `C_i(t)`

with intervention and delayed-decision dynamics.

### 2.4 Simplified update structure (interpretation layer)
For interpretability, we summarize the dynamics as a locally coupled update:

1. local node dynamics produce provisional next state
2. neighbor coupling injects disturbance/cognitive influence
3. optional activation gating selects contributing neighbors
4. optional nonlinear amplification and feedback modify effective input
5. targeted intrinsic decay damps disturbance-related dimensions

Conceptually:

- `D_i(t+1) = F_D(local_i, neighbors_i, gating_i, feedback_i, decay_i)`
- `C_i(t+1) = F_C(local_i, neighbors_i, gating_i, feedback_i, decay_i)`
- `T_i(t+1), S_i(t+1)` evolve through local governance/intervention terms and coupling-mediated effects

This interpretation does not replace the implementation; it provides a compact map for the observed propagation behavior.

### 2.5 Propagation and ignition definitions
Run-level propagation is defined by strict conjunction:

- affected share >= 20% (non-seed nodes)
- cascade depth >= 5
- sustained failure >= 3 consecutive steps

Ignition is defined here as a regime of sustained global activation (high activation fraction maintained over time), not transient spikes.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Baseline and weak-coupling behavior
Multiple baseline and weak-coupling families contain configurations with propagation share near or equal to `1.00`. Local propagation is therefore easy to trigger in parts of the tested space.

### 4.2 Structural importance of targeted decay
Applying decay indiscriminately across state dimensions produces collapse-like artifacts. Restricting intrinsic decay to disturbance-related dimensions yields meaningful threshold behavior and stable comparative regimes.

### 4.3 Fine threshold evidence (PC 100-run scan)
From [governance_topology_compare_decay_targeted_pc_summary.csv](governance_topology_compare_decay_targeted_pc_summary.csv):

- `k_C = 0.02`: propagation share spans `0.39 -> 0.01` across `alpha = 0.076..0.085`
- `k_C = 0.03`: propagation share spans `0.51 -> 0.02` across the same alpha range

Threshold windows are topology-dependent, not universal.

### 4.4 Gated/feedback/hysteresis suppression
Right-side gated/feedback families show strong suppression:

- right-gated scans: propagation share `0.00..0.03`
- right feedback/hysteresis scans: propagation share `0.00` in reported grids

### 4.5 Interpretation
Propagation and ignition are distinct regimes. In tested configurations, propagation can occur without robust sustained global ignition.
These results are consistent across repeated runs and multiple experimental families.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Structural implication
Local coupling can propagate disturbance, but the resulting regime is strongly controllable by damping and activation-control structure.

### 5.2 Topology role
Topology changes quantitative outcomes (extent, speed, threshold location), but does not by itself force ignition in tested ranges.

### 5.3 Scope and reproducibility caveats
Conclusions are bounded by:

- tested parameter coverage
- finite tested network sizes
- fixed seed-node protocol in reported runs
- current metric conventions in the pipeline

These are scope boundaries, not contradictions.

### 5.4 Future work
Priority extensions:

- broader size scaling experiments
- seed-location sensitivity sweeps
- explicit nonlocal/global feedback channels
- alternative accumulation/state-memory mechanisms

## 6. Conclusion
Within this simulator family and tested ranges, local coupling supports propagation but does not robustly produce sustained ignition as a stable attractor. Threshold transitions exist and are topology-dependent, while targeted damping and gating-like mechanisms can strongly suppress propagation. The results indicate that additional structural ingredients beyond purely local coupling are likely required for robust ignition.
