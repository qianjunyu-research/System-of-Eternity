# Bounded Propagation Without Ignition:
# Structural Limits of Local Coupling in Governance Network Simulations

## Abstract
We investigate disturbance propagation in locally coupled governance network simulations across four topologies: chain, star, random-sparse, and fully connected. The implemented simulator evolves node trust, disturbance, stability, and cognitive distortion with local coupling, interventions, and stochastic updates. Propagation is classified with a strict rule requiring all three conditions: affected share >= 20% of non-seed nodes, cascade depth >= 5, and sustained failure >= 3 consecutive steps. Baseline and weak-coupling families often show high propagation, but targeted damping, gating, and nonlinear local mechanisms create strong suppression regimes. Fine-grained scans over intrinsic decay (alpha = 0.076..0.085) reveal topology-dependent flip windows rather than a single universal threshold. Within the tested model family and parameter ranges, we do not observe a robust sustained ignition phase as a stable attractor. The findings support bounded or suppressible propagation under local coupling and motivate future work on additional nonlocal structural mechanisms.

## 1. Introduction
Local interactions can produce rich cascades in networked systems, but it remains unclear when those cascades become sustained global ignition. This question is central in decentralized governance-like systems, where global behavior emerges from local rules.

This paper evaluates whether local coupling alone is sufficient for ignition in the implemented simulator family. We focus on three questions:

1. Does local coupling produce robust sustained ignition under tested conditions?
2. How does topology shift propagation extent and thresholds?
3. How do targeted local extensions (decay targeting, gating, nonlinearity, feedback, hysteresis) change regime boundaries?

Our conclusions are explicitly scoped to the tested simulator class and parameter ranges.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Baseline behavior
Baseline and weak-coupling families include many configurations with propagation share near or equal to `1.00`. This shows propagation can be easy to trigger under some local settings.

### 4.2 Decay targeting changes regime structure
Applying intrinsic decay indiscriminately across state dimensions produced collapse-like artifacts. Restricting intrinsic decay to disturbance-related variables produced meaningful threshold behavior and enabled stable regime comparisons.

### 4.3 Fine threshold evidence from PC-scale scan
In the 100-run targeted PC scan (`alpha = 0.076..0.085`):

- for `k_C = 0.02`, propagation share ranges from `0.39` down to `0.01`
- for `k_C = 0.03`, propagation share ranges from `0.51` down to `0.02`

Topologies do not flip at identical alpha values; threshold windows are topology-dependent.

### 4.4 Gating/feedback/hysteresis suppression regimes
Extended local mechanisms produce strong suppression in right-side parameter families:

- right-gated summaries: propagation share in `0.00..0.03`
- right feedback/hysteresis summaries: propagation share `0.00` in the reported grids

### 4.5 Interpretation
Propagation and ignition are distinct regimes. Propagation can occur without robust sustained global ignition.

Within tested configurations in this study, we do not identify a robust sustained ignition phase as a stable attractor.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Structural implication
Local coupling supports propagation but does not guarantee ignition. Quantitative outcomes are strongly shaped by damping and activation-control structure.

### 5.2 Topology role
Topology modulates propagation extent and threshold location, but does not by itself force ignition in the tested ranges.

### 5.3 Reproducibility and scope caveats
Results are reproducible within the provided code and datasets, but conclusions are limited by:

- finite parameter coverage
- finite network sizes in tested families
- fixed seed-node protocol in reported runs
- metric design choices in the current pipeline

These limits do not invalidate the findings; they define their scope.

### 5.4 Future work
Future extensions should test:

- broader size scaling
- seed-location sensitivity sweeps
- explicit nonlocal/global feedback channels
- alternative state accumulation mechanisms

## 6. Conclusion
In this simulator family, local coupling produces propagation that is often bounded or suppressible under targeted local mechanisms. Fine-grained decay scans reveal topology-dependent threshold windows rather than a universal ignition point. The absence of robust sustained ignition in tested regimes suggests that additional structural ingredients beyond purely local coupling are likely required.
