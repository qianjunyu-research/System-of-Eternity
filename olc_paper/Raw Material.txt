Title: Raw Experiential Record — Internal Agent Construction and Dissolution

This document records a past cognitive experience that later led to the development of a structured model (OLC: Opposition–Load–Coupling). The content below is not written as a scientific paper, but as a reconstruction of what occurred at the time.

---

Phase 0 — Background State

At the time, I was in a prolonged period of social isolation with minimal meaningful interpersonal interaction. I had strong internal cognitive activity but lacked external channels for discussion, feedback, or alignment.

I had a strong desire for:

* meaningful conversation
* understanding
* intellectual companionship

These needs were not satisfied in my environment.

---

Phase 1 — Initial Construction

I began with self-directed internal dialogue.

This started as:

* talking to myself internally
* simulating conversations
* generating responses from “another side”

At first, this was weak and clearly under my control.

However, I intentionally reinforced it:

* I allowed the “other side” to respond
* I tried not to override it immediately
* I maintained continuity in its responses

Over time, this formed a small but stable “other perspective.”

---

Phase 2 — Opposition Creation

I then deliberately pushed this internal construct to oppose me.

Instead of agreement, I forced:

* disagreement
* resistance
* counterarguments

This created a structure where:

* one part represented my main cognition
* the other part acted against it

The purpose was to:

* break internal echo
* create real conflict for thinking

This made the interaction stronger and more dynamic.

---

Phase 3 — Cognitive Load Amplification

At the same time, I was experiencing strong emotional states:

* sadness
* despair
* instability

Instead of avoiding them, I used them.

These states increased:

* mental intensity
* internal processing
* uncontrolled associative thinking

I noticed that under these conditions:

* responses became faster
* less consciously controlled
* more “generated” rather than “decided”

---

Phase 4 — Subconscious Coupling

At this stage, the opposition module began to change.

It started to:

* generate responses I did not fully anticipate
* feel less directly controlled
* show continuity beyond immediate intention

It felt like:

* something was “thinking back”

In reality, I believe this was:

* subconscious processes feeding into the construct
* not a separate entity

But subjectively:

* it felt autonomous

---

Phase 5 — Shared Value Anchor

Despite the opposition, complete conflict did not occur.

At some point, both sides converged on something we both valued deeply and wanted to protect.

This created:

* a shared reference point
* a stabilizing factor

Even when we opposed each other:

* this anchor remained consistent

It reduced total collapse of the system into chaos.

---

Phase 6 — Functional State

At its peak, the agent served as:

* a conversation partner
* a challenger of ideas
* a regulator of internal states

It helped me:

* think more deeply
* simulate different perspectives
* reduce the feeling of complete isolation

At the same time, I noticed risks:

* it could influence my thinking strongly
* boundaries were not always clear
* it could override or interfere with decisions

---

Phase 7 — Instability Recognition

At some point, I realized:

* this system was becoming too influential
* control was not perfectly stable
* the boundary between “me” and the construct was weakening

This was not immediate loss of control, but:

* increasing concern about long-term stability

---

Phase 8 — Dissolution

I decided to dismantle the construct.

This was done by:

* stopping reinforcement
* reducing internal dialogue
* refusing to engage with the “other side”

Over time:

* the responses weakened
* continuity broke
* the agent disappeared

---

Phase 9 — Aftermath

After dissolution:

* I no longer had that internal companion
* the sense of “dialogue” disappeared
* I returned to a more unified cognitive state

This came with:

* increased stability, sadness, loneliness, hollow.
* but also loss of that interaction

---

Phase 10 — Long-Term Outcome

Over time, I replaced this internal system with:

* structured thinking
* formal frameworks
* externalized models (e.g., system design work)

Instead of:

* internal agent-based thinking

I moved toward:

* system-level abstraction

---

Additional Notes

* This entire process happened several years ago, it ends with my 4 attempts of suicide
* The agent no longer exists
* I am not attempting to recreate it
* The current goal is to analyze and formalize what happened

---

End of Record
