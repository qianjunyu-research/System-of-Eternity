Model Limitations
•  Results are constrained to the tested parameter ranges.
•  Recovery capacity is modeled as stable and does not deplete over time.
•  Trust dynamics are simplified and do not include hard threshold-collapse behavior.
•  Noise is modeled as independent rather than correlated or systemic.
•  The simulated population is homogeneous rather than agent-diverse.
•  Horizon is limited to 100 steps.
•  Twenty runs per condition limits precision of probability estimates.
•  Branch does not yet simulate: right of exit, peaceful replacement, interoperability loss, or ADPIE-style governance evaluation loops.
Revised Stability and Failure Laws
These laws describe the current model branch only — not universal SOE behavior.
Law 1 — Threshold + Reinforcement
Stability requires threshold crossing plus state-dependent reinforcement. Crossing the cooperation threshold (~0.80) alone is insufficient; without a lock-in mechanism, the system oscillates and fails to stabilize.
Law 2 — Stability is a Regime
Stability is a regime, not an average value. Systems must remain above threshold continuously. Average cooperation above threshold does not imply stability.
Law 3 — Bounded Parameter Region
Within the tested range, stability appears to exist inside a bounded parameter region. Outside this region, stability degrades progressively.
Law 4 — Disturbance as Primary Limiter
Within the tested configurations, disturbance was the strongest observed limiting factor. Disturbance determines the outer boundary of stability.
Law 5 — Structural Resilience
Structural support improves resilience but does not erase disturbance sensitivity. Increasing structural_weight cannot fully compensate for high disturbance levels.
Law 6 — Recovery-Dependence
Recovery dynamics preserve operation under stress. The system is recovery-dependent rather than disturbance-free.
Law 7 — Gradual Degradation (within tested range)
Within the tested ranges, degradation appeared gradual and no catastrophic collapse was observed. This applies to the tested parameter window only.
Law 8 — Cognitive Distortion as Secondary Axis
Cognitive distortion is a meaningful secondary stress axis once coupled to trust. Without the trust pathway, it is effectively null.
Law 9 — Trust–Recovery Coupling
The trust–recovery loop is a critical dependency. Trust degradation weakens recovery capacity, creating a vulnerability pathway.
Law 10 — Compound Stress
Combined stressors produced the strongest degradation observed. Precise interaction strength remains under-controlled pending matched single-axis reruns on v2.
Law 11 — Primary Failure Pathway
The most severe degradation observed occurred under volatility combined with weakened trust — the configuration that most directly disrupts the recovery loop.
Law 12 — Robustness Boundary
Even under multi-axis stress, recovery remained functional. However, the system approaches the failure boundary under compound stress — robustness has limits not yet fully mapped.
Core Principle
The modeled system remains stable when it preserves the trust–recovery loop and operates within its disturbance tolerance range.
These laws describe the current model branch, not universal SOE behavior.

Core Definitions
Term
Definition
Collapse
Failure to restore system cooperation above 0.80 within 100 years after a disruption event. Means functional collapse within the recovery window, not extinction or permanent inability to recover.
Survival
The system remains structurally intact and retains the ability to recover cooperation, even if recovery is delayed.
Recovery
Restoration of cooperation above the 0.80 threshold within the defined time window.
Functional Collapse
Recovery occurs after the allowed time window, or cooperation remains below threshold too long to preserve effective system operation.
Governance Survival Threshold
governance_stability >= 0.50. Values below 0.50 indicate systemic governance collapse risk in the Codex macro simulator.
Structural Fragmentation
Loss of network unity measured through rising connected-component counts or declining largest-component ratio.

Section C  —  Agent Simulations (Claude)
CL-FORM-1  —  Cooperation Emergence Model
Cooperation emerged naturally in 9 of 10 scenarios without top-down enforcement.
Starting from as low as 0.40 mean cooperation, the network self-organized above the 0.80 threshold by year 285.
The main failure mode was the reciprocal waiting trap: too many conditional cooperators stalled the network below threshold.
CL-FORM-2  —  Trust Dynamics Experiment
Betrayal creates a persistent new equilibrium rather than a temporary dip.
Large betrayals and heavier infiltration push cooperation below the 0.80 threshold and do not naturally recover.
Trust builds slowly and breaks quickly, indicating the need for an active trust recovery mechanism.
CL-FORM-3  —  Time-Horizon Cooperation Test
Planning horizon is decisive: horizon 20 reached final cooperation 0.9887, while short-horizon regimes failed badly.
Mixed populations show that short-horizon nodes drag long-horizon nodes downward.
Abundance alone did not repair this problem; long-horizon norm formation remains necessary.

Section H  —  Behavior × Topology Integration  (CL-NET Series)
Section H Synthesis Conclusion
The CL-NET series demonstrates that the SOE Trust Recovery Mechanism is conditionally effective. Recovery succeeds only when trust decay is non-zero and network topology supports distributed coordination. Under persistent adversarial pressure and low decay conditions, passive recovery fails and the system enters functional collapse. Active coordination mechanisms are therefore required for reliable recovery.

Section Y  —  Execution Layer Constraints (v1.0)
Trust Recovery
Passive decay alone is insufficient under persistent adversarial pressure. Recovery requires active propagation or coordination mechanisms.
Temporal
Systems fail functionally if recovery does not occur within the defined time window, even when eventual recovery is possible.
Topology
System behavior depends on network structure. No topology is universally optimal, and centralized topologies require stronger recovery capacity.
Parameter Sensitivity
System stability exists only within bounded parameter ranges such as trust decay, coordination speed, and event load.
Adversarial Persistence
Continuous adversarial pressure prevents passive recovery and forces active regeneration mechanisms.
Section X  —  Core System Laws (v1.0)
Synthesis: These laws represent the consolidated results of the SOE simulation program across behavioral, topological, adversarial, and multi-model validation layers.
1
Permanent trust damage suggests functional collapse.
In the tested CL-NET regime, if trust memory does not decay, timely recovery becomes impossible.
2
Recovery must occur within a critical time window.
Systems that recover too slowly enter functional collapse even when eventual cooperation is possible.
3
Defense without recovery leads to fragmentation.
Isolation of adversaries preserves local integrity but does not by itself restore cooperation.
4
Trust propagation contributes to recovery but is not sufficient on its own.
Signals must combine with trust adaptability and timing.
5
Centralization increases recovery requirements.
Highly connected hubs raise fragility under disruption unless redundancy is present.
6
System stability is parameter-dependent.
Survival depends on trust decay, topology, coordination speed, and event load.
7
Recovery requires both trust decay and coordination.
Neither mechanism alone was sufficient across the tested matrices.
8
Trust recovery has real-world cost constraints.
Propagation and repair must be efficient enough to avoid exhausting the system before recovery completes.
Core Principle: A system survives if it prevents permanent trust damage and restores cooperation within a critical time window.
Architectural implication: a viable SOE-style system needs trust adaptability, distributed coordination pathways, and recovery mechanisms that act before the functional-collapse window closes.
These laws are conditional system laws derived from model-constrained simulations and depend on topology, timing, event load, and parameter regimes.
