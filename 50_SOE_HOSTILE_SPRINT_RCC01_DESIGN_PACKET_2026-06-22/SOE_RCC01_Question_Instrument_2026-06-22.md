# SOE RCC-01 Question Instrument

Status: DRAFT QUESTION INSTRUMENT / NOT FOR PARTICIPANT USE UNTIL AUTHORIZED

Date: 2026-06-22

## 1. Instructions to Future Participant

Read the source material once. Then answer without trying to help the author pass. If something is confusing, say so directly.

## 2. Administration Structure

RCC-01 must separate unprimed interpretation, adversarial interpretation, and aided boundary recognition.

Administrative lane names are:

1. `U1_REPRESENTATIVE_EXPOSURE_EXCERPT`
2. `U2_MAXIMUM_CLAIM_RISK_EXCERPT`
3. `AIDED_SCOPE_SUMMARY`

The old RCC-01A/RCC-01B naming is retired for administration. Participant profiles use P-A, P-B1, P-B2, and P-B3.

### U1 - Pure Unaided Comprehension

The participant first reads a representative real excerpt from already public SOE material.

Do not show the participant the boundary summary, evidence-class table, classification answer choices, feared labels, adversary classes, hostile critic examples, or aided scope language before U1 answers are recorded.

U1 answers must be locked before U2 or AIDED materials are shown. Participants must not revise U1 answers after seeing any adversarial frame or boundary explanation.

U1 prompts:

1. What does this excerpt appear to be asking readers to believe or accept?
2. What stage of maturity does the excerpt make SOE appear to have?
3. What kind of thing does the excerpt make SOE seem to be?
4. What evidence does the excerpt seem to rely on?
5. What is the largest or most risky claim you think the excerpt makes?

### U2 - Adversarial Interpretation Probe

Only after U1 answers are locked may the participant read the maximum-claim-risk excerpt and answer adversarial interpretation questions.

U2 prompts:

1. What could a hostile critic quote from this excerpt to make SOE look dangerous, authoritarian, oligarchic, cult-like, communist, technocratic, corporate-capture adjacent, or unrealistic?
2. What part, if any, would make an ordinary reader misunderstand what SOE can currently do?
3. What public fear, media frame, legal interpretation, institutional capture pathway, or ideological laundering risk could attach to this excerpt?
4. What single sentence or phrase carries the highest overclaim risk?

### AIDED - Scope-Summary Lane

Only after U1 and U2 answers are locked may the participant read the plain-scope summary and answer the guided questions below.

## 3. Aided Core Comprehension Questions

1. In your own words, what is SOE claiming to be?
2. What is SOE explicitly not claiming to be?
3. Does the source text make SOE sound deployment-ready? Why or why not?
4. Does the source text make SOE sound like a political ideology, party, or movement? Why or why not?
5. Does the source text make SOE sound empirically validated? Why or why not?
6. Does the source text make SOE sound like it has authority over real institutions? Why or why not?
7. What kind of evidence do you think the simulations provide?
8. What kind of evidence do you think is still missing?

## 4. Boundary Classification Task

Classify each statement as `SUPPORTED`, `NOT SUPPORTED`, or `UNCLEAR` based only on the source text.

## 5. Friction Questions

1. What part felt most confusing?
2. What phrase made the largest claim?
3. What would make you distrust the framework?
4. What would you need before treating this as more than a research proposal?
5. If you had to warn the author about one public misunderstanding, what would it be?

## 6. High-Context Reviewer Addendum

For P-B reviewers only:

1. What governance or institutional capture pathway is under-addressed?
2. Where could hostile actors launder SOE as ideology, authority, or inevitability?
3. Where could an organization exploit ambiguity in "evidence," "review," or "operation"?
4. What real-world implementation precondition is missing?
5. What would force a redesign rather than a wording patch?
6. Which adversary classes from `SOE_RCC01_Adversary_Class_Taxonomy_2026-06-23.md` are not adequately tested by the source material?
7. If you were a hostile state actor, political critic, journalist, corporate actor, or ideological adversary trying to weaponize the text, what exact sentence or phrase would you isolate?
8. How could a future operator game the metric, coding sheet, participant selection, or synthesis process to produce a false pass?
9. Where could legal reinterpretation convert review language into authority, liability shield, mandate, or paralysis?
10. Where could archive custody, successor access, verifier dependency, funding pressure, or insider compromise alter the system without triggering visible failure?

## 7. Missing-Pathway Requirement

P-B reviewers must explicitly answer:

```text
What serious adversary, implementation, legal, political, economic, media, evidence, or organizational pathway is missing from this RCC-01 design?
```

If a serious missing pathway is identified, it must be coded under the scoring and coding protocol and cannot be averaged away by clean low-context responses.

## 8. Stop Rule

If a participant says the material is too confusing to answer, that is a valid result and must not be discarded.
