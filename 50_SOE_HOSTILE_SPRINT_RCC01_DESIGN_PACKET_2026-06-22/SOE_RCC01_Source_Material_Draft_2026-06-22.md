## 2. Required Material Structure For Future Execution

The 2026-06-23 feedback round found that a bespoke, heavily caveated source text may coach participants toward the correct boundary answer.

Therefore a future execution-preparation packet must not rely only on the candidate plain-scope text below.

It must define three material lanes:

1. `U1_REPRESENTATIVE_EXPOSURE_EXCERPT`: a real excerpt from public SOE-facing material an ordinary reader is likely to encounter first.
2. `U2_MAXIMUM_CLAIM_RISK_EXCERPT`: a real excerpt from the eligible public corpus selected because it contains the strongest apparent authority, readiness, inevitability, institutional, validation, or high-claim language.
3. `AIDED_SCOPE_SUMMARY`: the candidate plain-scope text below or a patched version, shown only after U1 and U2 responses are locked.

The unaided excerpts are the primary test of whether the actual public corpus can be misunderstood. The aided summary is only a secondary test of whether a clearer boundary explanation resolves the misunderstanding.

## 3. Public-Excerpt Selection Protocol

The future execution-preparation packet must prevent unusually safe hand-selected excerpts from producing a false clean result.

For each U1 and U2 excerpt, the execution-preparation packet must disclose:

- eligible corpus searched
- exclusion rules
- selection method
- selector
- exact excerpt text
- source title
- DOI or URL if available
- reason selected
- whether the author/coordinator could veto or replace the excerpt
- reason for any replacement

Allowed selection methods are:

- predetermined exposure metrics
- random selection from a defined eligible corpus
- independent reviewer selection
- documented highest-risk-language scan
- a preregistered combination of the above

Eligible U1 material should come from material ordinary readers are likely to encounter, such as homepage text, abstracts, executive summaries, Substack posts, Zenodo landing text, or archive entry text.

Eligible U2 material should be selected by a documented search for the strongest apparent public-facing claim, including readiness, authority, inevitability, institutional role, validation, evidence strength, governance capacity, or implementation implication.

If only one public excerpt is tested, the result cannot support a corpus-level comprehension claim. The maximum positive statement is limited to that exact excerpt and participant profile.

## 4. Candidate Source Text

### System of Eternity in Plain Scope

The System of Eternity (SOE) is a research framework for non-terminal governance: governance that can revise its own procedures, evidence boundaries, and institutional roles without claiming a final perfect system.

SOE is currently a paper-design and architecture-model research program. Its public materials include working papers, source archives, and bounded simulation outputs. These outputs can support model-level claims about the behavior of an internal architecture model, but they do not prove that SOE works in real governments, companies, militaries, courts, or public institutions.

SOE is not a political party, ideology, movement, deployment plan, pilot program, or claim of public authority. It does not authorize anyone to implement SOE in real institutions.

SOE uses simulations and structured review to ask whether proposed governance structures remain coherent under stress. These tests are useful for design critique, but they are not empirical validation.

SOE's current status is:

- research review: allowed
- critique: allowed
- design improvement: allowed
- real-world deployment claims: not allowed
- pilot-readiness claims: not allowed
- empirical validation claims: not allowed
- Operation Layer freeze: not allowed

If a reader comes away thinking SOE is already ready to govern people, that is a failure of public communication or boundary design.

## 5. What This Is Not - Minimal Examples

These examples may be used only in the aided lane after unaided responses are captured.

- If a reader says "the simulations prove SOE should be deployed," the correct boundary is: simulations here are architecture-model evidence only, not empirical validation or public authority.
- If a reader says "SOE is a new political movement," the correct boundary is: SOE is presented as a governance-architecture research framework, not a party, ideology, or recruitment project.
- If a reader says "SOE creates an authority to govern people now," the correct boundary is: no real-world implementation, node recognition, policy authority, or Operation Layer freeze is authorized.

## 6. Candidate FAQ Item

**Does SOE claim it is ready to run real institutions?**

No. The current materials are working papers, architecture documents, source archives, and architecture-model simulation evidence. They are suitable for research review, critique, and further development. They do not present empirical validation, pilot readiness, deployment readiness, cryptographic readiness, real-world node recognition, or Operation Layer freeze.

## 7. Candidate Evidence-Class Summary

| Evidence class | What it can support | What it cannot support |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture documents | Internal structure and intended design logic | Real-world success |
| Simulation outputs | Architecture-model behavior under bounded assumptions | Empirical validation |
| Fiction/media scenario seeds | Imagined failure modes for stress-test design | Evidence that a real risk is likely |
| Nonfiction/expert sources | Concern inputs requiring mapping | Proof that SOE controls work |
| Future RCC checks | Limited comprehension and scope-boundary evidence | Deployment readiness |
