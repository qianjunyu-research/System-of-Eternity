# SOE Logo Failure Modes And Rejected Directions

This file records what the artist should avoid based on prior AI exploration and multi-AI review.

## Rejected As Final: Broken Circle O With Bite-Like Gap

Problem:

- The O looked bitten off.
- The mark felt visually awkward rather than elegant.
- The concept was defensible, but the execution did not feel good enough for public use.

Why it matters:

- A mark can be conceptually correct and still aesthetically wrong.
- SOE needs institutional confidence, not a clever diagram.

## Rejected As Final: Four-Gap Ring

Problem:

- Four equal gaps create crosshair or reticle associations.
- It can also read as a loading spinner or UI status icon.
- This is especially risky because SOE is a governance framework and must avoid military, targeting, or operational-authority signals.

## Rejected As Final: Side-Gap O

Problem:

- A strong side gap can make `SOE` read as `SCE`.
- Readability must come before symbolism.

## Rejected As Final: Center Dot Underline

Problem:

- A centered dot under a circular O can imply target, coordinate, or ritual mark.
- It may still work in some refined lockups, but it should not be assumed.

## Rejected As Final: AI Geometry As Logo

Problem:

- Some AI-generated marks looked like diagrams, concept sketches, or system icons rather than a real identity.
- Human refinement is needed for typography, spacing, optical balance, and emotional register.

## Lessons To Preserve

The failed explorations still taught useful constraints:

- non-terminal openness is important,
- the mark should not close into rigid finality,
- a small controlled interruption can be meaningful if it remains beautiful,
- the identity should stay quiet and serious,
- full wordmark readability matters more than symbolic cleverness.
- heavy symbolism can hurt the mark.

## Best Current Direction

Use a human-designed, wordmark-first SOE identity.

The final solution may use:

- no symbol,
- a subtle typographic intervention,
- a non-circular open structure,
- a line/gate/threshold motif,
- a refined O that remains clearly an O.

The designer should not be trapped inside the circle solution.

The designer should also not be trapped inside the idea that the logo must visibly explain "non-terminal governance." A light cue is enough. A strong mark is better than a clever mark.
