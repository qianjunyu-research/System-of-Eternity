# SOE Logo Review Rubric

Use this rubric when reviewing human artist proposals.

## Verdict Options

```text
ACCEPT_SOE_LOGO_DIRECTION
PATCH_SOE_LOGO_DIRECTION
REJECT_SOE_LOGO_DIRECTION
```

## Core Questions

1. Does the mark clearly read as SOE or System of Eternity?
2. Does it look credible for a serious civilizational governance research framework?
3. Does it avoid startup, crypto, military, political, cult, and sci-fi signals?
4. Does it work in one color?
5. Does it work at favicon and avatar size?
6. Does it fit the Non-Terminal Research website identity?
7. Does it feel durable enough for papers, archives, and long-term use?
8. Does it express openness/revision/non-finality without looking broken?
9. Does the design feel aesthetically strong, not merely conceptually clever?
10. Would QianJun Yu be comfortable using it publicly for years?
11. Does it avoid trying to carry too much theoretical meaning?
12. Does it avoid obvious visual confusion with existing institutions, universities, public agencies, standards bodies, political organizations, military marks, commercial brands, or stock-logo templates?

## Automatic Patch Triggers

Patch is required if:

- the mark reads as `SCE`,
- the mark looks like a loading spinner,
- the mark looks like a target, reticle, scope, or coordinate marker,
- the mark feels like an app icon or crypto token,
- the mark feels like a political campaign,
- the mark uses a mystical seal or cult-like geometry,
- the typography is generic or weak,
- small-size readability fails,
- the design only works in color,
- the mark looks like a diagram rather than an identity.
- the explanation is stronger than the visual result.
- the mark creates plausible confusion with an existing institution, public agency, university, standards body, political mark, military mark, commercial brand, or commonly used stock-logo template.

## Automatic Rejection Triggers

Reject if:

- SOE is not readable,
- it looks militarized,
- it looks like a nationalist/political emblem,
- it looks like a religious or esoteric movement,
- it looks like a game faction,
- it looks like a startup product logo,
- the designer cannot provide usable rights/source files,
- the mark is based on stock/logo-template art.
- the mark is knowingly derived from, or materially confusing with, an existing protected identity.

## Acceptance Standard

Accept only if the mark is both:

```text
visually strong
```

and

```text
conceptually appropriate
```

Concept alone is not enough. A simple, memorable mark with light meaning is better than a heavy symbolic system that looks awkward.
